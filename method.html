How verdicts work

Last updated 12 October 2026

Guidance, not a fatwa

HalalLens gives guidance based on a product's ingredient list, its certification and your settings. It is not a fatwa, a religious ruling or a halal certificate. For a ruling, ask a scholar you trust.

The three verdicts

Halal: every ingredient is recognised as halal for your settings, or a certifier you trust has certified the product. Doubtful: at least one ingredient's source is unknown, scholars differ on it, or we don't recognise it yet. Not halal: an ingredient is prohibited for your settings, such as pork, alcohol or gelatine of unknown source.

Where the rules come from

Each ingredient rule follows published sources: the Qur'an, numbered fatwas from recognised fatwa bodies (such as Darul Uloom Deoband, AskImam, Darul Iftaa Birmingham, MUI Indonesia and Muis Singapore) and halal standards, including the standard Pakistan uses (PS 3733, based on OIC/SMIIC 1). Every rule lists its sources on the ingredient page, and each source is checked against its published text. Where no published source settles a question, the app stays cautious and says Doubtful. The rules are not a fatwa and have not been reviewed individually by a scholar.

Schools of thought

Scholars differ on some ingredients, such as shrimp, shellfish, insect colours and cheese rennet. Choose your school in your profile and verdicts follow it. Each ingredient page shows how each school treats it.

Certification

Only a named certification body counts, for example JAKIM, MUI, HMC, HFA, SANHA or IFANCA, found on the pack or in the product's data, or a published brand certificate that covers the product. A plain "halal" word or tag does not count. You can choose which certifiers you trust.

Where product data comes from

Products are looked up in Open Food Facts, then USDA FoodData Central, then a saved copy in the app. If none has the ingredient list, photograph the label: the text is read on your phone and you can correct it before it is checked. Recipes change, so always read the pack in your hand.

Why some products stay Doubtful

We never guess a hidden source. An emulsifier like E471 can be plant or animal; unless the pack says "vegetable" or a trusted certifier covers it, it stays Doubtful. That is the honest answer, and the ingredient page tells you what to check.
